WHO GIVES A DAMN?
By Zhan Petrov

Who gives a damn?

Who sleeps with whom?

Air Force One was headed for Beijing in May when Marco Rubio told Donald Trump that U.S. intelligence had linked NVIDIA’s CEO to a suspected Chinese spy. That’s what The New York Times and The Washington Post reported.

The plane stopped for fuel in Anchorage. Jensen Huang got on.

Six months later, a reporter asked Trump if he knew about it at the time. “No, I didn’t hear about it then,” he said.

According to the Times, Rubio’s warning was at least the second time Trump had been briefed.

Apparently the president’s memory had a longer layover than the plane.

The Times also reported a separate relationship between Huang and an NVIDIA executive who reported directly to him.

Alice Huang had been an executive at Singapore-based chip buyer Megaspeed.

The Times and the Post reported that U.S. intelligence concluded she worked for Beijing—an allegation China’s foreign ministry rejected.

The Information, citing people who know her, an NVIDIA China sales employee, and corporate records, reported that her relationship with Jensen Huang helped get at least $7.5 billion worth of NVIDIA chips through intermediaries to Chinese firms.

NVIDIA’s board said on November 8, when it opened its inquiry, that it had no reason to believe national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised. Fine.

Who signed off on the shipments?

Where did the chips go? What did NVIDIA tell investors about the customer?

On Wednesday, November 11, The Wall Street Journal and Reuters reported that NVIDIA’s board was preparing to oust Huang. As of early November 12, the company had yet to announce a final decision.

The stock fell more than 8 percent in Wednesday’s after-hours trading.

Now consider a hypothetical scenario: a 30 percent crash across the wider market would take a much bigger chain reaction—say, a hypothetical pullback where a cloud company pushes its next chip order into next year, and a lender demands actual revenue before funding another data center.

That’s how a boardroom scandal could start to look like the dot-com bust.

Meanwhile, Wall Street spent Wednesday evening, November 11, dumping NVIDIA. On Thursday, November 19, if the IPO goes ahead as scheduled, it gets to buy Anthropic.

Read the November 10 prospectus before you call that a change of heart. At the assumed midpoint price, NVIDIA itself has agreed to buy about $10 billion of non-voting Anthropic stock in a concurrent private placement contingent on the IPO.

The board can show Jensen Huang the door.

Somebody still has to sign the check.
